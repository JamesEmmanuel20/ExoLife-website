Cosmic Detective Work: Finding Invisible Worlds

Exoplanets don't emit their own visible light—they are blindingly outshone by their host stars. Discover the ingenious physics techniques astronomers use to find them across trillions of miles.

Most Productive

1. Transit Photometry

When a planet crosses directly between its star and our telescope, it blocks a tiny fraction of starlight. By measuring this repeating dimming dip over time, astronomers calculate the planet's size and orbital period.

  • Best for: Close-in planets & space telescopes (Kepler, TESS)
  • Reveals: Planet radius & orbital year
Mass & Weight

2. Radial Velocity (Wobble)

A planet's gravity isn't just pulled by the star—the planet also pulls back! As an exoplanet orbits, it causes its host star to wobble slightly back and forth. We detect this using the Doppler shift of starlight.

  • Best for: Massive worlds & ground observatories
  • Reveals: Planetary mass & gravitational pull
Visual Snapshots

3. Direct Imaging

Using specialized optics called coronagraphs and starshades, telescopes block out the blinding glare of the host star so we can capture actual direct photographs of giant outer planets.

  • Best for: Young, massive planets far from their stars
  • Reveals: Direct thermal glow & atmospheric colors
Einstein's Relativity

4. Gravitational Microlensing

When a foreground star and its exoplanet pass in front of a distant background star, their gravity acts like a cosmic magnifying glass, bending and temporarily brightening the starlight.

  • Best for: Rogue planets & distant galactic worlds
  • Reveals: Worlds light-years away in deep space
