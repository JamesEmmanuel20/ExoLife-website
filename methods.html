Cosmic Detective Work: Finding Exoplanets

Because planets are millions of times fainter than their host stars, astronomers use ingenious physics and high-precision optics to detect them across light-years.

🌑

1. Transit Photometry

When a planet crosses (transits) directly in front of its star from our viewpoint, it blocks a tiny fraction of starlight. Telescopes measure this periodic dimming.

Used by Kepler, TESS & JWST
〰️

2. Radial Velocity

A planet's gravity causes its host star to wobble slightly back and forth. This stellar wobble shifts the color of the star's light via the Doppler effect.

Measures Planetary Mass
📸

3. Direct Imaging

Using specialized starlight blockers called coronagraphs, telescopes blot out the blinding glare of a star to snap direct photographs of giant outer worlds.

Best for Young Gas Giants
🔍

4. Gravitational Microlensing

Einstein’s theory of relativity shows that massive objects bend starlight. When a foreground star and planet pass in front of a distant background star, they act like a cosmic magnifying glass.

Finds Distant Rogue Worlds
